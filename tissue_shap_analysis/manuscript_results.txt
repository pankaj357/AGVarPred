Cell-type-specific functional genomics signals underlying AGVarPred predictions

We investigated whether AGVarPred's predictions are driven by a small number of cell types or distributed across diverse tissues and assays. Of the 111 AlphaGenome features selected by the model, we parsed 78 distinct biosamples and assigned each to one of 13 broad tissue/lineage groups (see Methods for parsing and grouping rules).

The top five tissue groups accounted for 67.9% of total AlphaGenome SHAP importance, and the top ten groups accounted for 91.8%. The highest-contributing tissue group was Brain / CNS (22.0%), followed by Liver / digestive (19.7%), Heart / vasculature (9.8%), Blood / immune (8.4%), and Reproductive (8.1%).

At the level of individual biosamples, the strongest contributors were HepG2_EFO_0001187 (8.5%), ascending_aorta (4.4%), testis (3.9%), H1_hESC (3.7%), and camera_type_eye (3.6%). These top five biosamples alone contributed 24.1% of AlphaGenome SHAP importance.

The assay-type breakdown differed across tissues. For Brain / CNS, the dominant assay type was SPLICE_JUNCTIONS (33.1% of that tissue's SHAP contribution). For Liver / digestive, the dominant assay type was CHIP_TF (43.2% of that tissue's SHAP contribution). For Heart / vasculature, the dominant assay type was SPLICE_SITE_USAGE (35.1% of that tissue's SHAP contribution). 

These results indicate that AGVarPred does not rely on a single generic functional signal. Instead, it draws on a broad but uneven distribution of cell-type-specific annotations, with a concentration in tissues relevant to development, proliferation, and gene regulation.
